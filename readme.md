# [Bearing Fault Diagnosis]Spectral Amplitude Modulation: Nonlinear Filtering Process for Diagnosing Rolling Bearings [with MATLAB Code]

Rolling bearings are key components of every rotating machine, and their failure is one of the main reasons for machinery shutdown or malfunction. For this reason, various early diagnostic methods have been proposed in the past. Among them, envelope analysis and cyclic spectral analysis (CSA) are the most effective and widely applied methods, which remove undesirable components based on the principle of linear filtering process of signals. However, in many cases, the machine structure may be subjected to impacts from defects during meshing at different frequency ranges, so it is necessary to filter the signal over multiple frequency bands to fully extract the defect signal. Moreover, finding the appropriate demodulation frequency band is not always easy. To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes an experience-based and automated nonlinear filtering process that decomposes the signal based on the power of different component parts. Then, the square envelope spectrum of these components is calculated to find the presence of bearing characteristic frequencies. Therefore, this method can be considered as a complement to narrowband amplitude demodulation technology. The phase of each filtering component is similar to the original signal's phase but with a changed amplitude. The idea is that only by reconstructing the signal through its phase can many useful features be retained. The use of retrospective whitening for bearing diagnosis also adopts a similar approach to reduce the strong influence of external sources masking the bearing signal, although its simplicity has already achieved significant results. Finally, the performance of the proposed method was verified on real case data recorded on two test benches. In addition, its effectiveness was studied under constant speed and variable speed conditions as well as in the presence of different levels of Gaussian and non-Gaussian (highly pulsed) background noise.
1. Introduction: As a critical component of every rotating machinery, the diagnosis of rolling element bearings (REB) is an important task. Because detecting faults before any catastrophic failure can save time and money.
Diagnostic information related to REB failures is often obscured by interference from more powerful sources. Envelope analysis [1] is the most famous method for extracting azimuth signals from raw signals and has become the benchmark technique for its diagnostics. It was first proposed in [2] and was named High Frequency Resonance Technology (HFRT), but it is now primarily known under its previous name. However, the main challenge is finding an effective frequency band for demodulation. Since then, various methods have been developed to overcome this challenge, such as the Fast Kurtosis Plot [3], Peak Plot [4], and Auto-Peak Plot [5], [6] to achieve valuable results. These methods select filtering time signals, filtering signal square envelope spectrum (SES) of the filtered signal, and filtering time signal envelope autocorrelation respectively with the highest degree of peakiness in the frequency bands. The main drawback of these techniques is associated with selecting a single frequency band for demodulation. This is problematic, especially when multiple defect features exist in different frequency bands, because in such cases, only one defect is detected while ignoring other defects. [5] proposed Combining Square Envelope Spectrum (CSES) as a solution to this problem.
Rotating machinery condition monitoring employs a second set of methods, namely cyclostationary spectral analysis (CSA) [7]. This method is a powerful tool that separates and describes different second-order cyclostationary components based on the carrier frequency and the modulation frequency variables. Spectral correlation (SC) is one of the main tools in machine signal CSA, defined as the two-dimensional Fourier transform (FT) of the instantaneous autocorrelation function. Discussions were made on various proposed SC estimation methods. High computational costs are the primary drawbacks of these methods, hindering their application in rotating machinery condition monitoring. Reference [9] presents a new method that includes cyclostationary modulation spectrum (CMS), which is a rapid alternative to SC, where SC is an envelope spectrum cascaded over all possible frequency bands. As a unified form of SC estimator, it serves as a cyclic steady state estimator. However, CMS has a major disadvantage because it cannot detect cycles beyond the STFT frequency resolution. Recently, a fast algorithm for computing SC was introduced, significantly enhancing computational efficiency [11]. Moreover, it is not affected by the limitations of CMS.
All the above techniques can be considered as linear transformations. Inverse cyclostationary analysis, as an alternative nonlinear transformation, has been used since Ref. [12] for various purposes such as speech analysis, mode analysis, gear and REBs diagnosis [13]. The advantage of this method lies in its ability to detect periodicity in the signal spectrum by simplifying the entire harmonic series into a single inverse cyclostationary line. Randal and Swahili [14] and Borghesani et al. [15] have utilized inverse cyclostationary analysis features to propose inverse cyclostationary editing and inverse cyclostationary prewhitening methods, respectively, to separate periodic and random components as well as signal prewhitening.
In Section 2 below, the theoretical background, advantages, and disadvantages of inverse spectrum analysis, inverse spectrum editing, and inverse spectrum preprocessing will be explained. Subsequently, in Section 3, a new empirical method based on these underlying principles is proposed. This method includes a nonlinear filtering process that is entirely different from existing REBs diagnostic methods. In Section 4, this method is implemented in six cases from two different experiments. Each data set is selected based on unique characteristics such as high Gaussian or non-Gaussian background noise or varying state speeds. In addition to the performance of the proposed method in these scenarios, detailed explanations of these aspects will be discussed. Finally, in Section 5, conclusions are drawn.
